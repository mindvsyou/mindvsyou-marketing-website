CBSE Pattern

CBSE Class 10 board exam pattern explained

6 min read · Updated Sep 2026

CBSE has moved steadily toward competency-based assessment over the past few years — testing whether you can apply a concept, not just recall it. That shift shows up directly in how the Class 10 question paper is structured. Here's what to expect, in general terms, and where to check the exact scheme for your exam year.

Two components: internal assessment + the board exam

Your final Class 10 result isn't based on the board exam alone. CBSE splits marks between internal assessment (conducted by your school — periodic tests, portfolio, subject enrichment activities, practicals where applicable) and the board exam itself, which is held externally and carries the larger share of the weightage. Your school will confirm the exact split and internal assessment components for your subjects — this varies slightly by subject.

The question types you'll see in the board paper

Within the board exam paper, questions are typically grouped into a few recognisable formats:

  • MCQs (Multiple Choice Questions) — objective-type questions, often including assertion-reason pairs, usually worth 1 mark each.
  • VSA (Very Short Answer) — brief, typically 1–2 mark answers that need a short explanation or calculation, not just a single word.
  • SA (Short Answer) — usually 2–3 marks, requiring a more complete explanation, a small derivation, or a short structured response.
  • LA (Long Answer) — typically 4–5 marks, testing a full explanation, multi-step numerical, or an essay-style response depending on the subject.
  • Case-based / source-based questions — a passage, scenario, or data set followed by several linked sub-questions, testing application rather than recall. These now appear across most subjects, not just Social Science.

The exact number of questions and marks assigned to each type is published by CBSE in the official sample question papers ahead of every academic year, and can shift slightly year to year — so always cross-check the current sample paper for your exam year rather than relying on last year's split.

Why the shift toward application-based questions matters for how you prepare

The practical effect of this pattern is that memorising definitions and formulas is necessary but not sufficient. A growing share of every paper expects you to apply what you know to a scenario you haven't seen worded that way before — which means the best preparation isn't just reading the chapter again, it's practising with questions that force you to apply the concept, the way case-based and PYQ-style questions do.

Where to check the exact current-year scheme

For the precise marks distribution, question counts, and any changes for your specific exam year, always refer to:

  • The official CBSE sample question papers and marking scheme, published on the CBSE Academic website ahead of the academic year.
  • Your school's subject teachers, who receive circulars on any pattern changes directly from CBSE.

Once you know the pattern for your year, the fastest way to get comfortable with it is practising real previous year questions in each format — MCQ, VSA/SA/LA, and case-based — so nothing on exam day feels unfamiliar.
