How schools can build a daily practice culture without extra burden on teachers

7 min read · Updated Sep 2026

Every school leader wants their Class 10 batch practicing more. Almost none of them want to hand their teachers another grading queue to get there. The good news: those two goals aren't actually in tension, if the setup is right.

The real obstacle isn't motivation — it's friction

Students already have a device in their pocket that can hold their attention for hours. The gap isn't that they're incapable of daily practice; it's that nothing currently asks them to, in a way that's as low-effort as everything else competing for that same fifteen minutes. Any practice initiative that requires an app download, a registration form with a dozen fields, or a lab booking is competing on the wrong terms — it loses to the path of least resistance every time.

The fix isn't more motivation. It's removing friction until daily practice is genuinely the easiest thing a student can do with a spare few minutes.

What low-friction actually looks like

One link, not a rollout

The fastest way to get a whole batch practicing is a single shared link or QR code that students open, register on in under a minute, and start using immediately — no individual account creation by the school, no IT department involved, no waiting on approvals. Share it once in the class group and students are in.

Self-checking, not teacher-checking

Anything that lands on a teacher's desk to grade has a ceiling on how much of it will actually happen, because teacher time is the scarcest resource in the system. Practice that gives instant feedback — right/wrong on MCQs, a model answer to self-rate against for written responses — removes teachers from the loop entirely while still giving students the feedback that makes practice useful.

Fits into a routine that already exists

You don't need a new period on the timetable. A two-minute mention during morning assembly, a pinned message in the class WhatsApp group, or a "before you leave today" reminder is enough to keep a practice habit alive — because the practice itself takes place on the student's own time, on their own device.

What to expect in the first few weeks

Adoption is rarely instant across 100% of a batch — a portion of students engage immediately, and the rest tend to follow once they see classmates mentioning it or see it become a normal part of the daily routine. Sharing the link consistently for the first couple of weeks, rather than a single one-off announcement, tends to matter more than any other single factor in how many students actually stick with it.

Print-ready practice for offline days

Not every day is a screen day, and not every student has reliable data access at home. A printable worksheet — questions plus an answer key, generated in seconds for any chapter — covers homework, a device-free study hall, or students without consistent internet, without asking teachers to build worksheets from scratch.
