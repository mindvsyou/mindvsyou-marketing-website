Chapter-wise vs full-paper PYQ practice: which is better for CBSE Class 10 Science?

6 min read · Updated Sep 2026 · By the MindVsYou team

Chapter-wise practice and full-paper practice are not rivals. They answer different questions. One tells you what you know; the other tells you whether you can perform on the day.

Quick answer

Start chapter-wise, finish with full papers. Chapter-wise PYQs build and diagnose knowledge while you are still covering the syllabus. Full papers, done under timed conditions in the final weeks, build stamina, timing and paper strategy. Skipping either one leaves a gap.

What is the difference between chapter-wise and full-paper PYQ practice?

Chapter-wise practice means solving only the PYQs from one chapter (or one subtopic) at a time, across several years. Full-paper practice means attempting a complete board paper start to finish, usually within the exam's time limit.

What are the strengths of chapter-wise PYQ practice?

  • Immediate feedback on what you just studied. You find gaps while the chapter is still fresh.
  • Pattern recognition. Seeing many questions on one topic together shows you how the board tests it: numericals, diagrams, definitions, reasoning.
  • Clean data. Your accuracy per chapter shows exactly where to revise.
  • Low pressure. You can do ten minutes on the bus.

The weakness: because you know which chapter the question is from, you skip a real exam skill, working out what the question is about.

What are the strengths of full-paper PYQ practice?

  • Timing. You learn how long each section takes and where you tend to overrun.
  • Mixed recall. Questions from different chapters arrive in a jumble, like the real exam.
  • Strategy. Which section first, when to skip, how to reserve time for case-based questions.
  • Stamina and nerves. Three hours of concentration is a skill of its own.

The weakness: a full paper is a blunt diagnostic. A poor score tells you that something is wrong, not what.

Which should you do first?

StageBest modeWhy
While covering the syllabusChapter-wiseLocks in each chapter and reveals gaps early.
Syllabus mostly doneChapter-wise for weak chapters, plus a first full paperTargets weak spots and gives a baseline.
Final 3 to 4 weeksTimed full papers, each followed by reviewBuilds timing and exam-day habits.

How should you review a full paper?

Do not just total the marks. Sort every lost mark by chapter and by reason (concept gap, misreading, slip, time). Then go back to chapter-wise sets for the chapters that leaked most. This loop, full paper to diagnosis to chapter-wise fix, is the most efficient use of PYQs. The step-by-step version is in how to practise Class 10 Science PYQs.

Frequently asked questions

Is chapter-wise PYQ practice enough for boards?

Not by itself. It builds knowledge well, but you also need timed full papers to develop pacing and mixed-chapter recall before the exam.

How many full papers should I attempt before boards?

Two to four timed papers in the final weeks is a reasonable target, provided you review each thoroughly. Reviewing matters more than the count.

Should I attempt full papers in a single sitting?

Yes, at least a few times, at the same time of day as your exam. It trains concentration and pacing.

Which is better for revision, chapter-wise or full paper?

Chapter-wise for revising specific weak areas; full papers to check overall readiness.
