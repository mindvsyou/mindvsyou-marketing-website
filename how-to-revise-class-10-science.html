How to revise Class 10 Science: combine revision and practice for boards

7 min read · Updated Sep 2026 · By the MindVsYou team

Most students revise by rereading, and most students feel like they are progressing when they are not. Effective revision is a loop of recalling, testing and fixing, and it looks a little uncomfortable because it is supposed to.

Quick answer

Revise Class 10 Science by recalling first, then testing yourself with questions, then fixing what you missed. Use a short summary for each chapter, attempt PYQs from memory before rereading, and revisit missed questions after two or three days and again after a week.

How do you revise Science effectively?

Effective revision has three moves: recall (write down what you remember before opening the notes), test (answer questions on it), and fix (return to the notes only for what you missed). Rereading alone only creates familiarity, which feels like knowledge. The reasoning is covered in why active practice beats passive reading.

What is a simple revise-then-test loop?

  1. Pick one chapter. Spend five minutes writing everything you remember: key terms, reactions, formulas, diagram labels.
  2. Check it against your summary or NCERT and note the gaps.
  3. Attempt ten to fifteen PYQs from the chapter without help.
  4. Review every miss and tag it: concept, misread or slip.
  5. Fix the concept gaps in your notes with a short line each.
  6. Re-attempt the misses after two or three days, then a week later.

Repeat across chapters in rotation, so every chapter comes round several times before the exam.

How do you combine revision with practice?

Do not schedule "revision days" and "practice days" separately. Merge them: each chapter's revision session ends with practice, and each practice session starts with a short recall. The result is that every hour both refreshes memory and finds gaps. PYQs are the best source of that practice, as they reflect the actual paper. See how to practise Class 10 Science PYQs.

How often should you revise each chapter?

A workable rhythm is: revise soon after learning, again after two or three days, again after a week, and again a few weeks later. You do not need to stick to it exactly; the idea is that gaps between revisions grow. Chapters you keep getting wrong should come round more often.

What should your Science revision notes contain?

  • Chemistry: reaction types with one example each, reactivity series, key properties, functional groups.
  • Physics: formulas with units, sign conventions, ray diagram rules, circuit symbols.
  • Biology: labelled diagrams, process flow (for example, the path of blood or food), key terms and hormones.

One page per chapter is enough. Longer notes just move the rereading problem.

How do you revise in the last few weeks before boards?

Shift the balance towards testing: chapter-wise sets for your weakest chapters first, then timed full papers. Avoid learning brand new material in the last days; focus on consolidating what you have and fixing repeated errors. See the wider plan in how to prepare Class 10 Science for boards.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best way to revise Class 10 Science?

Recall from memory, test yourself with board questions, then fix what you missed. Combine this with spaced repetition, revisiting weak chapters more often.

Is rereading NCERT enough for revision?

Rereading helps you refresh, but on its own it creates false confidence. Pair it with PYQs and self-testing to find what you actually cannot recall.

How many times should I revise the syllabus?

Aim for at least two or three full passes, with weak chapters revisited more often. Timing depends on how many weeks you have.

Should I make separate revision notes?

Yes, short ones. One page per chapter with formulas, key terms and diagrams is enough for quick recall.
