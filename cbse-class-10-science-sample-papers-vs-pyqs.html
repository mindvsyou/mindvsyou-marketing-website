Do you need to buy sample papers for CBSE Class 10 Science? Official SQP vs PYQs vs publisher books

7 min read · Updated Nov 2026 · By the MindVsYou team

Oswaal, Arihant, Educart, PW, Next Toppers: every year Class 10 students ask which sample paper book to buy. Our answer: yes, a good sample paper book is worth buying. But it helps only at the right time, and only if you solve it like the real exam, against the clock.

Quick answer

Yes, buy one sample paper book, once you have (1) completed the syllabus, (2) completed recent board PYQs and (3) solved the official CBSE Sample Question Paper (SQP) as a timed 3-hour mock test. CBSE releases only one official SQP per subject per year, so a publisher book (Oswaal, Arihant, Educart, PW and others) gives you the extra full-length papers you need for the final weeks. Sample papers only pay off when you solve them with time management: a timer, no book open and a full review afterwards.

What is an official CBSE sample paper?

Each academic year CBSE publishes one Sample Question Paper (SQP) for Class 10 Science, along with a marking scheme that shows the expected answer and how marks are split. For the 2026-27 session it was released in September 2026 on CBSE's academic website: CBSE Class 10 sample question papers 2026-27.

The SQP is the closest thing to an official preview of your board paper. It shows the current number of questions, the mix of MCQs, Assertion-Reason questions, short and long answers and case-based questions, the internal choices, and the marks for each. The marking scheme also shows which keywords and steps earn marks, which most students never read.

The "15 sample papers" or "10 sample papers" on a book cover are not from CBSE. Publishers write their own papers to match the SQP's pattern. That is useful, but it is not the same thing.

When should you buy a sample paper book?

A sample paper book is worth buying, because CBSE's own material runs out. There is only one official SQP a year, and once you have practised recent board papers you know those questions. A publisher book gives you 10 to 15 fresh, unseen papers in the current pattern, which is exactly what you need to keep taking full mock tests in the last weeks before the boards.

The timing matters. Buy and start the book once you have ticked all three:

  1. Syllabus complete. A full paper covers every chapter. If chapters are still left, you will get stuck, look up answers, and waste a fresh paper.
  2. PYQs complete. Recent board questions are what CBSE has actually asked. Finish them chapter-wise first, then as full papers. See how many years of PYQs to solve.
  3. Official SQP done as a timed mock. Solve CBSE's SQP in 3 hours and check it against the marking scheme. This shows you the exact pattern, so you can judge whether a publisher's papers match it.

One good book is enough. Three or four books cover the same syllabus and pattern, so the extra papers add volume, not new learning. Choose with the checklist below.

Sample papers only work with time management

A sample paper solved with the book open and no clock is just more chapter practice. Its real value is training you to finish an 80-mark Science paper in 3 hours, with time left to check. So solve every sample paper like the board exam:

  • Set a 3-hour timer and sit at a table, phone away, in one sitting.
  • Use the first 15 minutes to read the whole paper, as you will in the exam hall, and decide your order of attack.
  • Note the time when you finish each section. This shows you where your time actually goes, usually the long answers and case-based questions.
  • No textbook, no solutions, no skipping ahead to the answers. If you are stuck, mark the question and move on, as you would in the exam.
  • Keep the last 10 to 15 minutes for checking. Practise this habit now so it is automatic on exam day.
  • Review the same day or the next. Mark it against the solutions, list every mistake and every question that took too long, and re-attempt those two days later.

Two to three timed papers a week, each properly reviewed, does more for your board score than solving a paper every day without a timer.

Can't buy a book right now? The free practice stack

Phases 1 to 3 cost nothing, and if a book is not in your budget you can still prepare well with free material:

  • Official SQP and marking scheme: free on CBSE's Class 10 SQP 2026-27 page, with the marking scheme alongside.
  • Previous year board papers: download recent CBSE Class 10 Science question papers as PDFs, free and without a login, each with the official marking scheme answer.
  • Chapter-wise PYQ practice: at quiz.mindvsyou.io you can practise real board MCQs, written answers and case-based questions one chapter at a time, with instant feedback. See which chapters and years are covered.
  • Your NCERT textbook: the in-text and end-of-chapter questions are still the base that board questions are built on.

Free websites often re-host the official SQP, which is fine. Just remember that any other "sample papers" they offer are their own, not CBSE's.

If you buy one: how to choose a sample paper book

Oswaal, Arihant, Educart, PW (Physics Wallah) and Next Toppers all sell Class 10 Science sample papers, and students on forums recommend each of them. Students who like harder, more conceptual questions often mention Arihant. Students focused only on board-level practice often mention PW or Educart. Any of them can serve you well. Rather than following the loudest recommendation, check the book against this list:

  • Latest edition: it should say it follows the SQP for your exam year (for 2027 boards, the 2026-27 SQP). Old editions may follow an older pattern.
  • Pattern match: compare one paper with the official SQP. Same number of questions, same sections, same marks?
  • All question types: it should include Assertion-Reason and case-based questions, not just MCQs and long answers.
  • Step-wise solutions: answers should show how marks are split, like the CBSE marking scheme, not just the final answer.
  • Difficulty you can use: a harder book is good for strong students with time to spare. If you are still fixing basics, it can cost you confidence and time.

Whichever book you pick, use it the same way: timed, then reviewed, then re-attempted.

Frequently asked questions

How many official sample papers does CBSE release for Class 10 Science?

CBSE releases one official Sample Question Paper (SQP) for Class 10 Science each academic year, with its marking scheme, on cbseacademic.nic.in. Every other "sample paper" you see in a shop or on a website is written by a publisher or coaching brand, not by CBSE.

Is Oswaal enough for Class 10 Science sample papers?

One good publisher book is enough for most students, whether it is Oswaal, Arihant, Educart or PW. Start it after you have completed the syllabus, recent board PYQs and the official CBSE SQP as a timed mock, and solve every paper with a 3-hour timer.

Are free sample papers on websites official?

Only the paper and marking scheme on CBSE's own academic website (cbseacademic.nic.in) are official. Free sites often re-host that official SQP, which is fine, but any additional "sample papers" they offer are their own work.

How many sample papers should I solve for Class 10 Science?

After the official SQP and recent board papers, aim for two to three publisher sample papers a week until the exam, each solved in 3 hours under a timer and reviewed. Quality beats quantity: a paper solved without a timer or without review adds little.

Should I solve sample papers or PYQs first?

PYQs first. Practise chapter-wise PYQs while revising, solve the official CBSE SQP as a timed paper, then full board papers. Start a publisher sample paper book after that, once your syllabus is complete.
