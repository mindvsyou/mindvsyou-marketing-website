Case-Based Questions

Common case-based question mistakes in Class 10 Science (and how to avoid them)

5 min read · Updated Sep 2026 · By the MindVsYou team

Most marks lost in case-based questions are lost the same few ways. The good news is that each has a simple fix, and none requires learning more Science.

Quick answer

The most common case-based mistakes are answering before reading the whole case, ignoring numbers and named details, answering from memory without using the passage, over-writing, and not checking linked sub-questions. Fix them with the read, name, underline, answer and check routine.

What mistakes do students make in case-based questions?

1. Jumping to the questions

You scan for the answer and find something that looks right. But cases often include a detail that changes the answer. Fix: read the complete passage once before touching a sub-question.

2. Ignoring numbers, units and named details

A value, a condition or a named organism is usually there for a reason. Fix: underline them as you read and check that your answer uses at least one.

3. Answering from memory only

Writing a textbook definition that does not use the case earns partial marks at best. Fix: tie every answer to something in the passage.

4. Answering from the passage only

The reverse error: repeating what the case says without adding the underlying principle. Fix: add the concept or term that explains it.

5. Writing too much

Each sub-part is usually worth one or two marks. A paragraph wastes time and can introduce errors. Fix: match length to marks: one or two precise lines.

6. Not noticing that parts build on each other

A wrong assumption in part (i) carries into part (ii). Fix: after finishing, reread the case with your answers and check they tell a consistent story.

7. Panicking at unfamiliar contexts

A passage about an unusual device or situation can look new even when the concept is standard. Fix: ask "which chapter is this really about?" Strip away the story and the concept appears.

What does a good case-based routine look like?

Read the case. Name the concept. Underline numbers and nouns. Answer each part in one or two lines using both the passage and the principle. Re-check the chain. The full method is in how to solve case-based questions.

Frequently asked questions

Why do I lose marks in case-based questions even when I know the chapter?

Usually because of reading habits rather than knowledge: skipping details in the passage, answering from memory without using the case, or writing more than the marks require.

How long should a case-based answer be?

As long as the marks demand. Typically one or two lines per sub-part, each using a detail from the passage and the correct concept.

How can I get faster at case-based questions?

Practise several cases regularly. Reading speed and concept-spotting both improve with repetition, and a few timed attempts before the exam help.
