Science MCQs for Class 10 boards — the concepts you cannot skip

8 min read · Updated Sep 2026

CBSE Class 10 Science covers Physics, Chemistry and Biology in one paper, and with limited revision time, not every chapter deserves equal attention. Here's a practical map of where MCQs and case-based questions tend to concentrate, and how to revise each area efficiently.

Chemistry: reaction-heavy chapters reward pattern recognition

Chemical Reactions and Equations and Acids, Bases and Salts are foundational — MCQs here often test whether you can identify a reaction type (combination, decomposition, displacement, double displacement, redox) or predict a product, rather than just recall a definition. Metals and Non-metals follows a similar logic: questions frequently hinge on reactivity series and predicting what happens when a specific metal reacts with a specific compound. Carbon and its Compounds tends to test naming conventions and functional groups, which are easy marks once the pattern clicks but confusing until it does.

The efficient way to revise this section isn't rereading reaction types — it's working through enough practice questions that you start recognising a reaction type on sight.

Physics: numericals and case-based scenarios go together

Light — Reflection and Refraction and The Human Eye and the Colourful World combine conceptual questions with numericals (mirror/lens formula, magnification) and are a common source of case-based questions built around diagrams or everyday scenarios like why the sky is blue or how a lens defect is corrected. Electricity is numerical-heavy — Ohm's law, resistance combinations, and power calculations show up constantly as both MCQs and longer numerical questions, so fluency with the formulas matters more here than almost anywhere else in the paper. Magnetic Effects of Electric Current tends to test right-hand/left-hand rule applications and simple diagram-based reasoning.

Biology: process chapters need diagrams, not just definitions

Life Processes is the single densest Biology chapter — nutrition, respiration, transportation and excretion each carry their own vocabulary and diagrams, and MCQs frequently test whether you can label or interpret a diagram rather than just define a term. Control and Coordination (the nervous and endocrine systems) and How do Organisms Reproduce follow the same pattern. Heredity often includes short numerical-style reasoning around Mendelian ratios. Our Environment is comparatively lighter but reliably appears in case-based questions about ecosystems and food chains.

How to prioritise with limited time

If you're revising against the clock, the highest-yield approach is:

  • Start with the chapters above — they carry proportionally more weight across MCQs, numericals and case-based questions than the rest of the syllabus.
  • For each chapter, do a mix of straight MCQs and case-based questions, not just one format — the same concept gets tested both ways.
  • Redo the ones you get wrong. A wrong MCQ you never revisit teaches you nothing; a wrong MCQ you come back to a few days later is exactly what fixes the gap.

This chapter list reflects how the CBSE Class 10 Science syllabus is generally structured and weighted — always confirm against your current-year syllabus document, since CBSE occasionally adjusts specific topics within a chapter.
